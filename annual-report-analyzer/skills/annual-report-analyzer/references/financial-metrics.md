# Financial Metrics: Definitions and Interpretation

Use consistent definitions so memos are comparable across companies. Always state the fiscal periods and show the inputs.

## Contents
- Growth
- Profitability
- Cash flow
- Balance sheet and liquidity
- Capital allocation
- Valuation
- Sector adjustments

## Growth

- **Revenue growth (YoY)** = (Revenue this period − Revenue prior period) / Revenue prior period
- **CAGR** = (Ending value / Beginning value)^(1 / years) − 1. Use over three years when available.
- Separate organic growth from acquisitions and currency effects when the filing discloses them.

## Profitability

- **Gross margin** = Gross profit / Revenue
- **Operating margin** = Operating income / Revenue
- **Net margin** = Net income / Revenue
- **ROE** = Net income / Average shareholders' equity
- **ROIC** (approximate) = NOPAT / (Debt + Equity − Cash), where NOPAT = Operating income × (1 − tax rate)

Interpretation: direction matters as much as level. A 15% operating margin that has risen for three years tells a different story than a 15% margin that has fallen from 22%.

## Cash flow

- **Free cash flow (FCF)** = Cash from operations − Capital expenditures
- **FCF margin** = FCF / Revenue
- **Cash conversion** = FCF / Net income. Persistently below 1.0 deserves an explanation.
- **Stock-based compensation** is a real cost. Note FCF both before and after SBC where it is material.

## Balance sheet and liquidity

- **Net debt** = Total debt (including finance leases) − Cash and short-term investments
- **Net debt / EBITDA** = leverage. Above roughly 3x is elevated for most non-financial businesses, but tolerance varies by industry stability.
- **Interest coverage** = Operating income (or EBIT) / Interest expense. Below roughly 3x warrants attention.
- **Current ratio** = Current assets / Current liabilities
- **Debt maturity profile**: how much comes due in each of the next five years compared with cash and expected FCF.

## Capital allocation

- **Shareholder yield** = (Dividends + Net buybacks) / Market cap
- **Share count trend**: net of issuance from stock compensation, is the count actually shrinking?
- **Acquisition record**: goodwill added versus subsequent performance and impairments.
- Ask whether buybacks were done at sensible prices, or just to offset dilution.

## Valuation

Pick the multiples that fit the business, and compare to both the company's own history and its peers.

- **P/E** = Price / Diluted EPS (use forward estimates if credible sources exist, and label them)
- **EV/EBITDA** = (Market cap + Net debt) / EBITDA
- **EV/Sales** for high-growth or currently unprofitable companies
- **P/FCF** or **FCF yield** = FCF / Market cap
- **P/B** for banks and insurers, alongside ROE
- **Dividend yield** and payout ratio for income-oriented names

A simple sanity check is a reverse view: what growth and margins does the current price imply, and are those plausible?

## Sector adjustments

Generic ratios mislead in some industries. Adjust:

- **Banks and insurers**: focus on net interest margin, efficiency ratio, credit quality (charge-offs, reserves), CET1 capital, book value, and combined ratio. EBITDA and FCF are not meaningful.
- **REITs**: use FFO/AFFO rather than net income; watch occupancy, lease expirations, and debt costs.
- **Software and subscription**: note ARR or remaining performance obligations, net revenue retention, and rule-of-40 style growth-plus-margin measures where disclosed.
- **Cyclicals (energy, materials, industrials)**: judge through the cycle; peak-earnings multiples flatter and trough multiples scare.
- **Pre-profit companies**: emphasize cash runway (cash / quarterly burn) and dilution risk.
